This policy covers:

<ul>
<li>the Business Card by NFC.cool app for iPhone, iPad and Android, and its App Clip;</li>
<li>the card pages at mycard.nfc.cool, and at a team's own domain when a team connects one;</li>

</ul>

This policy also covers cards made in the NFC.cool Tools app and the contacts left on them.

Everything else in the NFC.cool Tools app, and the nfc.cool website, have their own [privacy policy](/privacy/).

## The data we process, and why

### Your account

When you create an account we store:

<ul>
<li>your email address and a username. The username is part of your card's web address, so it is public;</li>
<li>your password, stored only as a salted hash, never as text;</li>
<li>if you use Sign in with Apple: the user identifier Apple gives us and the email address Apple shares, which can be a private relay address;</li>
<li>whether you confirmed your email address, when you joined and when you last signed in;</li>
<li>which app you signed up in, and the customer identifier of your in-app subscription (see Subscriptions in the app);</li>
<li>the 4-digit PIN you can set to protect private cards. We store it encrypted, and decrypt it only to check it and to show it to you;</li>
<li>the date you started a team trial, if you did, because each person gets one trial.</li>
</ul>

The apps keep you signed in with a short access token (15 minutes) and a refresh token (180 days). The iPhone app keeps them in the iOS Keychain. The Android app keeps them encrypted on the phone and also in Google's Block Store, which can include them in your Google account backup, so you stay signed in on a new phone. We keep a record of each refresh token until it expires, so that "sign out everywhere" and a password change can end every session. The web console uses a session cookie.

<strong>Purpose:</strong> to give you an account, sign you in, keep it secure and contact you about it. <strong>Legal basis:</strong> performance of our contract with you (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

### Your cards

A card holds what you put on it: your name, photo, job title, company, department, a short bio and note, phone numbers, email addresses, postal addresses, websites, social media profiles, your birthday, logos, a header image, colours, and the title, subtitle and image of its App Clip card. The app also uploads the card as a vCard, which we store.

Each card is a web page at mycard.nfc.cool, and on a team card also at the team's own domain if the team connected one. Anyone who has the link, taps your NFC tag or scans your QR code can open it and save your details.

<ul>
<li><strong>Public card:</strong> listed in our sitemap. Search engines may index it.</li>
<li><strong>Private card:</strong> opens only with its PIN, and tells search engines not to index it. A link you share from the app can carry the PIN, so the person you give it to can open the card directly.</li>
</ul>

<strong>Purpose:</strong> to show your card to the people you share it with. <strong>Legal basis:</strong> Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

### People who view a card

When someone opens a card page or the App Clip, we record:

<ul>
<li>that the card was viewed, and when;</li>
<li>that the card was saved to contacts, and when;</li>
<li>that a link on the card was tapped, and when, and whether that was on the web page or in the App Clip.</li>
</ul>

These records hold no IP address, no device identifier, no cookie and nothing else about the visitor. The card's owner, and on a team card the team, see them as counts.

To deliver a page, our server receives the visitor's IP address and browser details, as every web server does. Our server logs keep them for a short time (see Technical data, errors and security). We also use the IP address to limit abuse, such as too many PIN attempts or contact form submissions: the counters are kept in our cache and expire after at most a day. Card images and page files are delivered by the content delivery network of our hosting provider, DigitalOcean.

<strong>Purpose:</strong> to show card owners how their card is used, and to protect the service and its users from abuse. <strong>Legal basis:</strong> our and the card owner's legitimate interests in those purposes (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

### Details people leave on a card (leads)

A card owner can switch on lead capture. A visitor can then leave their details with the card owner: first name, last name, email address, phone number, job title, company and a note, and, if the card asks for one, a selfie. The card decides which of these it asks for. This happens on the card page or in the App Clip.

When a selfie is sent, our server resizes it, adds the card owner's and the visitor's names and a logo, and stores it privately with the contact. It can be opened only through a short-lived signed link, given to the people who can see the contact.

<strong>Who is responsible for these details.</strong> The card's owner decides whether to collect contacts, which details to ask for and what to do with them. On a team card, the team decides. So the card's owner or team is the controller of these details, and is responsible for using them lawfully and for telling the people they collect them from what they do with them. NFC.cool processes them on the owner's or team's behalf (Art. 28 GDPR): we store them, deliver them as described below, and use them for no purpose of our own. For teams this is set out in our [data processing agreement](/terms/business-card/dpa/), and for everyone else in the [Business Card terms](/terms/business-card/).

After a contact arrives, the card owner, and on a team card the team, can add notes, tags and a status, assign the contact to a team member, export contacts as a CSV file, and on a team send follow-up emails.

When a contact arrives:

<ul>
<li>the card owner's phones get a push notification with the visitor's name. On a team card, the team's owners and admins (or the people the team chose) are notified too, with the name of the member who received the contact;</li>
<li>if the card owner runs Conference Mode, the visitor's name and company appear on its Live Activity;</li>
<li>on a team card, the details are sent to the team's webhooks, HubSpot or Salesforce, if the team connected them, and a follow-up email is scheduled if the team set one up.</li>
</ul>

<strong>Retention:</strong> a contact stays until the card owner or the team deletes it, or the card owner deletes their account. See How long we keep data for team contacts.

<strong>If you left your details on a card.</strong> The card's owner or team decides about your details, so ask them first. They can show you what they hold, correct it and delete it. If you cannot reach them, or they do not answer, email info (at) nfc (dot) cool with the card's link. We pass your request on to them and help them answer it. If a team is deleted, its owner chooses whether a contact assigned to one of its members moves to that member's own account, where that member becomes responsible for it, or is deleted with the team.

### Follow-up emails from teams

A team member can email a contact who left an email address, using the team's templates. A team can also send one automatically after a delay, to every new contact during an event of up to 7 days.

<ul>
<li>The email is sent by our email provider, Mailjet, from an NFC.cool address. It shows the member's name as the sender and sends replies to the member.</li>
<li>Every email has an unsubscribe link. After someone unsubscribes, the team can no longer email that address. If an email bounces or is reported as spam, the team can no longer email that address either.</li>
<li>We do not track whether a follow-up email was opened.</li>
<li>We keep a copy of each email sent (recipient, subject, text and delivery status) with the contact.</li>
</ul>

Only teams on a trial or a paid plan can send follow-up emails, and the number per member and per team is limited.

### Teams: NFC.cool Business Card for Teams

When someone sets up a team at business.nfc.cool we store the team's name, its brand (logos, colours, company name, website), and, if the team sets up an App Clip card, its place name, address and phone number. We store the team's members and their roles, invitations (the email address and name a team admin enters, and the card prepared for the person), requests to move a card into or out of the team, and the team's cards.

The email we send with an invitation says which team invited you and links to this policy.

<strong>Who is responsible for members' cards and invitations.</strong> The team decides what goes on the cards it makes for its members and whom it invites, so the team is the controller of these details. We process them on the team's behalf under our [data processing agreement](/terms/business-card/dpa/). Your own account (your email address, password, sign-in and settings) is ours to look after, as described in Your account.

An admin can create and edit cards for members and set their job title and department. A member can see and edit their own card within the limits the team sets.

When a member leaves the team or deletes their account, the team keeps the card and the contacts it collected. If the member deleted their account, we first remove their name, photos, contact details, birthday and PIN from the card. Their job title and department stay on it until the team changes them.

<strong>Team billing:</strong> teams pay through Stripe. Stripe collects the payment details, billing address and any tax ID on its own checkout page. We do not see card numbers. We send Stripe the team's name, the buyer's email address and our internal team identifier, and we store Stripe's customer and subscription identifiers, the plan, its status and trial dates.

<strong>Connections a team can switch on.</strong> These send data only when the team's admins set them up:

<ul>
<li><strong>Webhooks:</strong> for each new or changed contact we send its name, email, phone, job title, company, note, tags and status, the card's label, the email address of the member who holds the card, and the name and email address of the person it is assigned to, to an address the team chooses, for example a Zapier or Make hook. We keep a log of each delivery, with what was sent, for 30 days, or 90 days if it failed.</li>
<li><strong>HubSpot and Salesforce:</strong> each contact's name, email, phone, company and job title, its status where the team's account has a matching one, and a note with who captured it, its tags, its message and the team's notes with their authors' names, are created or updated in the team's own account. To assign the contact to the right person there, we look up the member's email address. We store the access tokens these services give us in encrypted form, and a delivery log for 30 days, or 90 days if it failed.</li>
<li><strong>Access tokens and AI assistants:</strong> an admin can create an access token for the team's API, or to connect an AI assistant (such as ChatGPT or Claude) through our MCP server. Within the token holder's role, the assistant can read the team's contacts, members, cards and their statistics, and can act for the holder, for example assign a contact, send a follow-up, invite a member or edit a card or the brand. Whatever the assistant reads is passed to the AI service the team chose, under that service's terms. We store only a hash of each token, and record each use (the token's name, what it did, when, and for some actions a short description) so admins can see it.</li>
<li><strong>Own domain:</strong> a team can serve its cards on its own domain. We check the domain's DNS records and request a TLS certificate for it from a public certificate authority.</li>
</ul>

<strong>Legal basis:</strong> performance of our contract with you and with the team (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). Where we handle the details of a person who acts for a team, such as its billing contact, our legitimate interest in running our contract with the team (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

The contacts a team's cards collect belong to the team: the team is their controller, and we process them on its behalf. Our [data processing agreement](/terms/business-card/dpa/) under Art. 28 GDPR is part of our terms and applies to every team. It lists the services we use for this.

You buy a plan through the App Store or Google Play. Apple or Google handles the payment, and we never see your payment details. The app uses RevenueCat to manage purchases, and our server asks RevenueCat which plan is active. We store the plan, the store it came from, the customer identifier and when the plan ends.

<ul>

<li>On iPhone, if you allow usage data (see Analytics and crash reports in the apps), RevenueCat also collects Apple's attribution token, which tells us whether the download came from an Apple Search Ads campaign. We use it only to measure our own ads. <strong>Legal basis:</strong> your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR).</li>
<li>If you answer the questions about your work when you first open the app (your industry, your networking goals, how often you network and what makes it hard), the answers are stored with your customer record at RevenueCat. We use them to understand who uses the app and to tailor the plans and offers we show you. The questions are optional. <strong>Legal basis:</strong> our legitimate interest in understanding our users and tailoring our offers (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). The answers are few and not sensitive. You can object at any time by emailing info (at) nfc (dot) cool, and we then remove them.</li>
</ul>

<strong>Legal basis</strong> for the purchase: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

### Analytics and crash reports in the apps

The iPhone and Android apps use the tools below. The App Clip, the card pages and the console use none of them.

<strong>Usage data, only if you allow it.</strong> The app asks you once whether you want to share usage data, with two buttons: Allow and Don't Allow. Until you answer, and if you choose Don't Allow, Firebase Analytics and PostHog stay off. You can change your choice at any time in More, under Share Usage Data. Turning it off stops them from then on, and on Android also clears the analytics data kept on your phone.

<ul>
<li><strong>Firebase Analytics (Google), iPhone and Android:</strong> records which screens you open and some actions in the app, such as opening the paywall, signing up, signing in, signing out and deleting your account, with an identifier for the app on your phone and device details. On Android it also records the steps of the first-run questions and your answers to them. Its advertising features are off.</li>
<li><strong>PostHog, iPhone, on servers in the EU:</strong> records app opens, screens viewed and recordings of how you use the app. Recordings hide the text, text fields and images on the screen, and your card, so they do not show the details on your cards or contacts.</li>
</ul>

<strong>Purpose:</strong> to understand how the app is used and improve it. <strong>Legal basis:</strong> your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), which is also the consent Portuguese law requires before an app stores or reads information on your phone for this purpose (Art. 5 of Lei 41/2004). You can withdraw it at any time, without affecting what happened before.

<strong>Crash reports, for everyone.</strong>

<ul>
<li><strong>Firebase Crashlytics (Google), iPhone and Android:</strong> when the app crashes, it sends a crash report with the device model, system version, app version, where in the app the crash happened, and an identifier for the app on your phone. Crash reports do not include your account or the details on your cards.</li>
</ul>

Crash reports are sent whatever you choose above, because we need them to keep the app working. They are strictly necessary for the app you asked for, so the law does not require consent for them. <strong>Legal basis:</strong> our legitimate interest in an app that works (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

<strong>App settings.</strong>

<ul>
<li><strong>Firebase Remote Config (Google), iPhone:</strong> when the app starts, it fetches settings we can change without an update, using an identifier for the app on your phone. We use it only for settings the app needs to work, not for experiments or to target you.</li>
</ul>

<strong>Legal basis:</strong> our legitimate interest in adjusting the app without an update (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Fetching these settings is needed to provide the app as you use it, so it does not depend on your choice about usage data.

The iPhone app never asks for permission to track you, so it cannot read your device's advertising identifier. The Android app does not ask for the permission to use your advertising ID.

A token is deleted when Firebase reports that it no longer works, and with your account.

<strong>Legal basis:</strong> Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR. You can turn notifications off in your phone's settings.

Conference Mode keeps your card's QR code on your lock screen as a Live Activity. To keep it up to date we store its push tokens, which cards it shows, your time zone offset, and any event you name with its start and end time and tag. New contacts appear on it as they arrive. When you sign out, its push tokens are removed.

<strong>Legal basis:</strong> Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

### Technical data, errors and security

<ul>
<li><strong>Server logs:</strong> our server logs each request (the address requested, with PINs and sign-in tokens removed, the time, the result, the IP address and browser, and if you are signed in, your account number) and every error. Sentry does not receive IP addresses, cookies, the contents of forms, contact details, email addresses, PINs or tokens. A report holds the technical details of the error, such as the address requested and the part of our code that failed.</li>
<li><strong>Rate limits:</strong> see People who view a card.</li>
</ul>

<strong>Purpose:</strong> to run the service securely and fix problems. <strong>Legal basis:</strong> our legitimate interest in a secure, working service (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

## Where your data is stored, and data outside the EU

Our server, database and files are hosted by DigitalOcean in its data centres in the New York area, USA. Mailjet stores emails in the EU, in Germany and Belgium. PostHog stores the iPhone app's analytics in the EU.

Where data leaves the European Economic Area, each transfer rests on one of these safeguards:

## Cookies

The card pages and the console use no advertising or analytics cookies, and load no third-party scripts. Fonts are served by us, through our hosting provider's content delivery network.

These are the only cookies set on them:

<ul>
<li><strong>csrftoken:</strong> protects forms, such as the contact form on a card or the console's forms, against forgery. It is set when you open a card or the console, and lasts about a year.</li>
<li><strong>sessionid in the console:</strong> keeps you signed in. It remembers which team you last opened and where you are in team setup, and briefly holds what the console shows you only once, such as a new access token. It lasts two weeks from your last visit, and ends when you sign out. A team's pages renew it, because the console writes to it there. Before you sign in, it is set only if a Sign in with Apple attempt fails, to show you the error.</li>
<li><strong>sessionid on a card page:</strong> set only if you enter a wrong PIN, or open a preview of your own card from the app. After a wrong PIN it counts your wrong PINs, and lasts five minutes from the last one. After three wrong PINs you wait those five minutes, and the cookie ends with the wait. A preview opens your card without its PIN for 30 minutes, and its cookie lasts two weeks.</li>
<li><strong>org_apple_nonce:</strong> when you sign in to the console with Apple, ties the sign-in to your browser. It lasts 10 minutes.</li>
<li><strong>__cf_bm:</strong> set by Cloudflare for DigitalOcean's content delivery network, which delivers the files and images of the console and the card pages. It tells people from bots, and lasts 30 minutes.</li>
</ul>

Counting a tap on a card's link sends no cookie. Signing in with Apple happens on Apple's own page, which sets Apple's cookies under Apple's policy. Paying for a team happens on Stripe's own page, which sets Stripe's cookies under Stripe's policy.

The console also keeps two things in your browser's storage. Your light or dark theme is stored only when you pick one, and stays until you clear it. During team setup, the last setup step shown is kept until you close the tab, so the progress bar moves the right way. Neither is sent to us.

<strong>Legal basis:</strong> these cookies are needed for the console and the card pages to work, which is why we do not ask for your consent.

## Security

All connections to our servers are encrypted with HTTPS, and browsers are told to use HTTPS only. Passwords are stored as salted hashes. PINs are stored encrypted. Access tokens for the team API are stored only as hashes, and tokens from HubSpot and Salesforce are stored encrypted. Selfies left with a contact and data exports are kept privately: they can be opened only through short-lived signed links, by the people allowed to see them. Admin access to our server requires two-factor authentication.

Photos, logos and header images on a card are public, like the card itself, and are delivered through DigitalOcean's content delivery network.

No system is completely secure, but we work to protect your data and fix problems quickly.
