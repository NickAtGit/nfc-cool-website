After years of building digital business card software, there was one problem that kept bothering me: we'd solved the wrong half.

The paper-card problem is real - cards go stale, fill your wallet, get lost, can't be updated. Digital cards fixed that. But they didn't fix the actual networking problem, which is much simpler:

> I meet 50 people at a conference, exchange info with 20, and three weeks later I cannot remember a single conversation.

The contact details on your phone are useless if you can't remember why that person is in your address book.

So I added what I've been calling the "memory upgrade" to NFC.cool Business Card: the Networking tab on iPhone. Pick anyone from your contacts, and you can write down what you'll want to remember:

- **Where and when you met.** The date, the event, the place and who introduced you.
- **What they're working on.** Their current projects, their expertise and where you could work together.
- **Conversation highlights.** The one or two things you actually talked about, plus any interests you share.
- **Follow-up plans.** "They're sending an intro to their VC." "Should send the deck on Monday."

That last bit connects to your calendar and reminders, because we're all bad at follow-through and we all need the nudge. Add Reminder and Add Meeting are right there on the contact.

---

## Why I do it right after the conversation

The trick is writing it down while the conversation is still fresh in your head. Five minutes later you've moved on to the next person. Three days later you don't remember whether the AI founder was the one from the Austin pitch competition or the Berlin hackathon.

So I save the contact, open the Networking tab and add the context before I walk over to the next person. The alternative - adding it next week from memory - never happens.

---

## What it changed for me

During beta testing across a few events, the experience shifted from "I have these business cards in my phone now" to "I have a queryable graph of people, what they do, and what I owe them".

I open the Networking tab in NFC.cool Business Card and see: who I met where, what we talked about, what I said I'd follow up on, what's still open. After meeting someone again, I update the entry - new conversation, new context. The card becomes a living record of the relationship, not a snapshot of contact details.

---

## Works however you met

The Networking tab starts from the contacts on your phone, so it doesn't matter how someone got into your address book: a tap on their card, a QR code at a noisy conference, or a paper card you typed in yourself.

Free and Personal include one card, Pro up to 10 (different roles, different events, different versions of you), and a team up to 10 per person. The context stays with the contact, so you keep it whichever card you shared.

---

## Why this matters now

The reason this didn't exist five years ago is that the bottleneck wasn't tech - it was friction. Capturing context required pulling out a separate notes app, typing while the other person watched, and then somehow associating those notes with the contact later. Most people gave up.

With [NFC.cool Business Card](/business-card/), the notes live right next to the contact, in the same app as your card. It's the difference between "I should remember this" and "this is now remembered".

In a world where we trade contacts faster than ever, the data that matters isn't who you know - it's why you know them.
