## If you want a free digital business card

**Wave Connect has a generous free plan.** [It](https://wavecnct.com/pricing) includes a digital card, unlimited sharing, Apple and Google Wallet, individual analytics and a lead capture form.

One thing to check. [Wave's FAQ](https://wavecnct.com/faq) says it adds no branding, but its pricing page lists "Remove Wave Connect branding" as a Pro feature. Those two pages don't agree, so look at your own free card before you rely on either.

Blinq and HiHello each offer two free cards and a free email signature. [Blinq's free plan](https://blinq.me/pricing) adds a contact scanner, and its name stays on your card until you pay for Premium. [HiHello's](https://www.hihello.com/pricing) gives you five scans a month.

NFC.cool's Free plan gives you one card, sharing by NFC, QR code or link, Save Contact, Conference Mode's QR code, Apple Wallet and a PIN. Paid plans add lead capture and analytics.

## If events are the reason you're buying

**Popl.** It calls itself an [AI GTM platform for event lead capture](https://popl.co/), and the product reads that way: a badge scanner it says works at any event, qualifying questions, contact enrichment, CRM sync and ROI reports. It says it's trusted by 90% of the Fortune 500.

The catch is the price. In its own words, "we quote pricing over a meeting instead of publishing it on our website". What it does promise is no per-seat charges and no usage fees. If you need a number before you talk to anyone, Popl can't give you one. It also has [a free plan and Pro plans for individuals](https://popl.co/pages/digital-business-card), but teams are clearly where its attention is.

Wave, Blinq and HiHello scan badges too, priced differently. Wave sells credits, at $2.50 each on the pack its pricing page shows by default, and a badge uses four. Blinq lists $9.99 per lead captured and enriched. HiHello Business includes 50 scans per user a month, pooled across the team.

NFC.cool works the other way around: Conference Mode pins your QR code to your Lock Screen on iPhone and Android, so people scan *you* without you unlocking your phone. On paid plans, views, saves and new leads count up right there, and you can tag every lead from an event.

## The privacy questions worth asking

Here are the questions I'd ask, plus what each company says about itself.

**Does it record conversations?** Blinq's [AI Notetaker](https://blinq.me/solutions/ai-notetaker) and Popl's AI Voice both record in-person conversations, and V1CE's AI plan includes a note taker for Zoom, Meet and Teams. That can be really useful. Blinq says plainly that you are responsible for getting consent from everyone you speak with, that it keeps in-person audio for 90 days on its free plan and for the life of your subscription on Premium or Pro, and that your recordings aren't used to train AI. NFC.cool doesn't record conversations.

**Does it look people up?** Most of the rivals here offer contact enrichment, which fills in details about the people you meet from other sources. Sales teams want exactly that. [Blinq's privacy policy](https://blinq.me/legal/privacy-policy) says it may collect information about people who aren't Blinq users, including from third-party data providers, and gives them a way to opt out. NFC.cool has no enrichment.

**Who contacts the people you meet?** Ask, and don't assume. Wave's FAQ says recipients "never get solicited by Wave". NFC.cool doesn't send marketing to people who view your card either. On Team, follow-up emails are your team's own: they go out under the sender's name "via NFC.cool", replies go to that person, and each one has an unsubscribe link.

**What happens to your data?** Wave, Blinq, HiHello and NFC.cool all say in writing that they don't sell it. On NFC.cool you can protect a card with a 4-digit PIN, keep a private card out of search engines, download everything as a ZIP and delete your account. The Business Card app has no ads.
